Program Notes

Where The Stars Shine Brightest is a piece that I wrote to encapsulate a poem I wrote. It reads:

Where the stars shine brightest is where I'll be.
Gazing at them, I wonder how I lived without this quiet.
Just me, my thoughts, and the stars.
Oh, how beautiful they are.
They twinkle, dance, and smile at me.
Even though they are millions of miles away, I feel so close to them.
Will this peace ever reach where I am now?
In the middle of this ever-expanding town, I'll never reach it.
The stars are so dim here, it's like they don't exist.
And for many here; they don't.
The cold black sky stares at them, if they dared to leave when the sun doesn't shine.
But for me?
I can still see them.
Wanting.
Hoping.
Caring.
Where the stars shine brightest is where I'll be.
For remember,
We will all join them one day.

My interpretation of the piece is related to mental health and the ideas of depression. I wrote the poem as a way to express how I was feeling during one of these times of hurt and sadness. The feeling of being “out of time” is something I took out of my experiences of feeling out of place, feeling insecure and hopeless. The “stars” in this piece represent hope, happiness, and comfort. They twinkle and gleam, shining through the “dark” black sky. As you might've guessed, the “stars” are represented by the higher pitched instruments, while the “dark” is represented through the lower pitched instruments.


This piece won WMEA Northlake Region Solo and Ensemble Composition Category in 2025.
